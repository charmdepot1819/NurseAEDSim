Choose a scenario

Pick a patient, then press Start AED. Your team does the CPR; you operate the AED.

How it works

CPR is already under way and your team keeps doing it. You are the nurse running the AED: switch it on, prepare the chest, choose and place the right pads, connect them, follow the voice prompts, keep everyone clear for analysis and shocks, and deliver the shock safely. Every step is timed and scored against the AHA 2025 and ERC 2025 guidelines, with a debrief at the end.

When the AED advises a shock, a “Stand clear!” prompt appears: call it, then press the flashing shock button. Use headphones or speakers to hear the AED.

AED operation you will be scored on
  1. Power on the AED as soon as it arrives
  2. Expose and prepare the chest (dry, shave, remove patches, bra)
  3. Choose adult or paediatric pads for the patient
  4. Place the pads: anterolateral or anteroposterior, clear of devices
  5. Connect the pads to the AED
  6. Hands off while the AED analyses the rhythm
  7. Shock advised: clear everyone, then press shock without delay
  8. Keep the AED on and the pads attached, even after signs of life

    Teaching points for this scenario

      Timeline

      AED prompts and what to do
      AED promptWhat you do
      “Apply pads to patient's bare chest.”The team continues CPR. Expose the chest; dry it, shave dense hair at pad sites, remove medication patches; peel and apply pads as pictured.
      “Plug in pads connector.”Push the pad cable firmly into the AED port (some models are pre-connected).
      “Check electrode pads.”Poor skin contact. Press pads firmly; dry or shave the skin, or replace the pads.
      “Analyzing heart rhythm. Do not touch the patient.”Compressions stop. Nobody touches the patient or the bed.
      “Motion detected. Stop motion.”Someone is still touching or moving the patient. Stop all movement.
      “Shock advised. Charging. Stay clear of patient.”Keep everyone clear. Look along the patient, and check oxygen is not blowing over the chest.
      “Press the flashing shock button now.”Call “Stand clear!” once more, look, then press the shock button without delay.
      “Shock delivered. Begin CPR.”The team restarts compressions immediately. No pause for a pulse check.
      “No shock advised. Start CPR.”The team restarts compressions immediately. A non-shockable rhythm (asystole or PEA) still needs high-quality CPR.
      “Stop CPR. Do not touch the patient.”2-minute cycle complete. Hands off for reanalysis. The team swaps compressors now to limit fatigue.

      Wording varies between manufacturers. These prompts follow the common pattern of AED trainers; always follow the device you have.

      Guideline quick reference
      PausesPre-shock and post-shock pauses as short as possible. Resume compressions immediately after a shock instead of pausing for a rhythm or pulse check.1,3
      When to use the AEDCPR until the AED is applied; attach it as soon as it is available. Pulse and breathing check: no more than 10 s.1,2
      Pad positionBare chest, anterolateral or anteroposterior. It may be reasonable to adjust a bra rather than remove it.1
      Children under 8Use paediatric pads or a dose attenuator if available; if not, a standard AED may be used.2
      Implanted deviceDo not place a pad over a pacemaker or ICD; keep at least 8 cm away or use an anteroposterior position.6
      Time to first shockIn-hospital VF/pVT, defibrillation delayed beyond 2 minutes is associated with lower survival to discharge.5
